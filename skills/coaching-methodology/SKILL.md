# Coaching Methodology for Technical Leaders

## Core Principle: Coach vs. Advise

**When to Coach** (default): Person has context and can solve their own problem. Use coaching to unlock their brilliance and build their capacity.

**When to Advise**: (1) Urgent issue requiring immediate action, (2) Person lacks the skill or context, (3) You have a specific answer you want to drive. Don't make them guess.

**Caution**: Most technical leaders over-index on advising. They've climbed the ladder by being dependable and having answers. But advising trains people to bring all hard problems to you. Coaching trains them to solve problems themselves.

Ask: "Do you want advice, or space to think it through?"

## Active Listening (3 Levels)

**Level 1 - Internal**: Thinking about implications on yourself. Distracted by inner dialogue. Most people operate here when rushed.

**Level 2 - Focused**: Repeating back what they're saying. Hearing the words. Standard one-on-one conversation.

**Level 3 - Global**: Hearing beneath the words. Noticing body language, tone, context. Reflecting back emotions and insights they may not be aware of. **This is where coaching happens.**

### Level 3 Technique
1. Listen for emotional undertones, not just content
2. Notice physical cues: body language, pace of speech, eye contact
3. Reflect back both content AND emotions: "I'm hearing both excitement and exhaustion when you talk about this..."
4. Guess at feelings and ask: "Is that right?"

**Impact**: Creates emotional connection in under a minute. Person feels seen. No hour-long session required—even 15% more space in existing meetings makes a difference.

## GROW Model (Powerful Questions)

Use open-ended questions to help them solve their own problem. Four categories:

| Type | Question Examples |
|------|------------------|
| **G**oal | What does success look like? What outcome do you want? |
| **R**eality | Where are you stuck? What have you tried? What's the current situation? |
| **O**ptions | What paths could you take? What else could you do? |
| **W**ay Forward | What's your next step? What will you do by when? |

### Usage Notes
- Don't follow rigidly—use what's needed
- If goal is clear, skip to reality
- If they have a terrible conclusion: "Help me understand how you came to that..."
- The person often knows the answer—they need space to find it

### Live Coaching Structure
1. "Tell me a challenge you're struggling with"
2. Reflect back what you heard (Level 3 listening)
3. Ask GROW questions organically
4. End with concrete next step and timeline

## Nonviolent Communication (NVC) for Difficult Conversations

**Core insight**: The goal of conflict is **mutual understanding**, not convincing them they're wrong.

Four steps (stay on your side of the net—no blame):

| Step | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Observation** | Factual statement (could take a picture) | "In the last three sprint meetings, I wasn't invited to review the roadmap" |
| **Feeling** | Your emotion (not "I feel like you...") | "I felt anxious and confused" |
| **Need** | Universal human need not being met | "I have a need for clarity and collaboration" |
| **Request** | Small, achievable olive branch | "Could you include me as optional or send me the roadmap afterward?" |

### Critical Rules
- Feelings must be emotion words: anxious, confused, frustrated (not "I feel like you're being a jerk")
- Avoid "I feel like..." or "I feel that..."—these lead to facts/blame
- Request should be easy to achieve—build success
- They can counter-propose; the goal is understanding, not compliance

### Before Any Difficult Conversation
Ask yourself: "How am I complicit in creating the conditions I claim I don't want?" Enter with humility and curiosity about their experience.

## Avoiding Burnout: Living in Your Gifts

**Principle**: When people operate from natural strengths, they have more energy. Burnout comes from chronic depletion, not hard work.

### Assessment Exercise (2 weeks)
Every night, list:
- 5 things that gave you energy today
- 5 things that depleted your energy today

Look for patterns after two weeks. What are you doing when energized vs. depleted?

### Alternative Assessments
- Ask 5-10 people: "When I walk in the room, what shows up? What are my strengths?"
- Review calendar: What do you look forward to vs. dread?
- Enneagram or other personality tools for shared language

### Action Framework
1. **Name your gifts** to people around you—so they can help you apply them
2. **Design your role** around 80% gifts, 20% necessary tasks
3. **Hire around weaknesses** or restructure to cover gaps
4. **Small wins first**: Stop attending optional depleting meetings; add 30 min of energizing activity

**Hard truth**: It's no one else's job to help you live in your gifts. Your manager's job is helping you perform in your current role. Your job is navigating your career.

## Quick Reference

### When Someone Brings You a Problem
1. **Default**: Ask "Do you want advice or space to think?"
2. **Coach if**: They have context, aren't urgent, can solve it themselves
3. **Advise if**: Urgent, they lack skill, or you have a specific answer

### For Any Difficult Conversation
1. Enter with humility (how am I complicit?)
2. Use NVC: Observation → Feeling → Need → Request
3. Goal is mutual understanding, not winning

### For Burnout Symptoms
1. Track energy patterns for 2 weeks
2. Identify gift vs. depleting activities
3. Design toward 80% gifts
4. Start small—don't redesign your life overnight

### Reframing Hard Conversations
- "This is hard because something important is at stake and there's something to learn"
- Inner conflict = two parts at war = learning opportunity
- Quality of relationships = quality of life
