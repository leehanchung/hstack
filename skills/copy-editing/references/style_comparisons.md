# Style Guide Comparisons

Comparison of major style guides for reference when copy editing.

## Major Style Guides Overview

### AP Style (Associated Press)
- **Primary Use**: Journalism, news writing, media
- **Key Features**: Concise, accessible, focuses on clarity for general audience

### Chicago Manual of Style
- **Primary Use**: Books, academic publishing, history
- **Key Features**: Comprehensive, detailed, offers flexibility

### MLA (Modern Language Association)
- **Primary Use**: Literature, humanities, liberal arts
- **Key Features**: Emphasizes authorship, literary analysis

### APA (American Psychological Association)
- **Primary Use**: Psychology, social sciences, scientific papers
- **Key Features**: Emphasizes currency of research, clear scientific communication

### AMA (American Medical Association)
- **Primary Use**: Medical and scientific journals
- **Key Features**: Technical precision, standardized medical terminology

## Key Differences

### Numbers

| Style Guide | Spell Out | Use Numerals |
|------------|-----------|--------------|
| AP | One through nine | 10 and above |
| Chicago | One through ninety-nine | 100 and above (flexible) |
| MLA | One through ninety-nine | 100 and above |
| APA | One through nine | 10 and above |
| AMA | One through nine | 10 and above |

**Exceptions across all styles:**
- Beginning of sentence: always spell out
- Ages, percentages, money: usually numerals
- Scientific/technical contexts: more numerals

### Oxford Comma

| Style Guide | Oxford Comma |
|------------|--------------|
| AP | No (unless needed for clarity) |
| Chicago | Yes |
| MLA | Yes |
| APA | Yes |
| AMA | Yes |

### Capitalization in Titles

#### AP Style (Title Case)
- Capitalize principal words
- Lowercase articles, conjunctions, prepositions under 4 letters

#### Chicago Style (Two Options)
- **Headline Style**: Similar to AP but more detailed rules
- **Sentence Style**: Only first word and proper nouns

#### MLA Style
- Capitalize all principal words
- Lowercase articles, prepositions, coordinating conjunctions

#### APA Style
- Capitalize words of 4+ letters
- Capitalize first word after colon

### Quotations

#### Punctuation Placement
- **American Style (all guides)**: Periods and commas inside quotes
- **British Style**: Logical placement (inside only if part of quote)

#### Block Quotes
| Style | Threshold | Format |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| AP | Rarely used | Indent, no quotes |
| Chicago | 100+ words or 2+ paragraphs | Indent, no quotes |
| MLA | 4+ lines | Indent 1 inch, no quotes |
| APA | 40+ words | Indent 0.5 inch, no quotes |

### Abbreviations

#### Titles
- **AP**: Dr., Mr., Mrs., Ms. (with periods)
- **Chicago**: Dr., Mr., Mrs., Ms. (with periods in American English)
- **Academic styles**: Generally spell out in text

#### Organizations
- **AP**: No periods (FBI, CIA, NASA)
- **Chicago**: No periods for well-known acronyms
- **Academic**: Spell out first use, then abbreviate

#### States
- **AP**: Two-letter postal codes (TX, NY)
- **Chicago**: Traditional abbreviations (Tex., N.Y.) or postal codes
- **Academic**: Usually spell out in text

### Time

| Style | Format |
|-------|--------|
| AP | 3 p.m., noon, midnight |
| Chicago | 3:00 p.m. or 3 p.m. |
| MLA | 3:00 p.m. |
| APA | Use 12-hour or 24-hour consistently |

### Dates

| Style | Format |
|-------|--------|
| AP | Jan. 15, 2024 |
| Chicago | January 15, 2024 or 15 January 2024 |
| MLA | 15 January 2024 |
| APA | January 15, 2024 |

### Em Dashes

| Style | Format |
|-------|--------|
| AP | Space — space |
| Chicago | No spaces—unless needed |
| MLA | No spaces—standard |
| APA | No spaces—standard |

### Lists

#### Bulleted Lists
- **AP**: Periods only if complete sentences
- **Chicago**: Flexible, consistency key
- **Academic**: Usually complete sentences with periods

#### Numbered Lists
- **All styles**: Generally use periods for complete sentences

### Citations

#### In-Text Citations
- **AP**: Attribution in text, no formal citations
- **Chicago**: Footnotes/endnotes or author-date
- **MLA**: (Author Page)
- **APA**: (Author, Year, p. #)

#### Bibliography Format
- **Chicago**: Bibliography or References
- **MLA**: Works Cited
- **APA**: References
- **AMA**: References with numbered citations

## Industry-Specific Preferences

### Business Writing
- Often follows AP for external communications
- May develop house style based on Chicago
- Emphasis on clarity and brevity

### Academic Writing
- Humanities: MLA or Chicago
- Social Sciences: APA
- Sciences: Often journal-specific based on AMA or similar
- History: Chicago (Notes-Bibliography)

### Web Writing
- Modified AP for readability
- Shorter paragraphs
- More subheadings
- Bulleted lists preferred

### Technical Writing
- Often custom style guides
- Emphasis on consistency
- May follow Microsoft Manual of Style or similar

## Regional Differences

### American vs. British English

#### Spelling
- -ize vs. -ise (organize/organise)
- -or vs. -our (color/colour)
- -er vs. -re (center/centre)
- Single vs. double l (traveling/travelling)

#### Punctuation
- Quotation marks: Double vs. single primary
- Period placement with quotes
- Serial comma usage

#### Vocabulary
- Elevator vs. lift
- Apartment vs. flat
- Gasoline vs. petrol

## Choosing a Style Guide

### Consider:
1. **Audience**: Academic, general public, professional
2. **Industry**: Publishing, journalism, academia, business
3. **Document Type**: Article, book, report, web content
4. **Organization Standards**: House style preferences
5. **Regional Preferences**: American, British, Canadian, Australian

### Best Practice:
- Choose one primary guide
- Document exceptions in style sheet
- Maintain consistency throughout document
- Consider hybrid approach for specific needs
